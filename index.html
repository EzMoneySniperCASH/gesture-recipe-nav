Garlic Butter Pasta

Serves
2
Time
20 min
Difficulty
Easy

Ingredients

  • 200 g spaghetti
  • 4 cloves garlic, thinly sliced
  • 4 tbsp unsalted butter
  • 2 tbsp olive oil
  • 1/2 tsp red pepper flakes
  • 1/4 cup pasta water (reserved)
  • Handful of flat-leaf parsley, chopped
  • Grated Parmesan, to finish
  • Salt and black pepper

Step 1 — Boil the pasta

Bring a large pot of well-salted water to a rolling boil. Add the spaghetti and cook to one minute shy of the package directions (you'll finish it in the pan). Before draining, scoop out about a cup of starchy pasta water and set it aside.

Tip: the pasta water is liquid gold for the sauce — don't skip reserving it.

Step 2 — Bloom the garlic

While the pasta cooks, set a wide skillet over medium-low heat. Add the olive oil and butter. As soon as the butter is foamy, add the sliced garlic and red pepper flakes.

Cook gently for 2–3 minutes, stirring often, until the garlic is pale gold and smells nutty. Do not let it brown — burnt garlic will turn the whole dish bitter.

Step 3 — Build the sauce

Lower the heat. Pour in about a quarter cup of the reserved pasta water and swirl the pan to emulsify with the butter. The sauce should look glossy and slightly thickened, like a loose cream.

Season with a pinch of salt and a few cracks of black pepper. Taste and adjust — it should taste a little under-seasoned at this point because the pasta will absorb more.

Step 4 — Toss everything together

Use tongs to lift the pasta straight from the pot into the skillet (a little extra water clinging to it is fine). Toss vigorously over medium heat for about a minute so the noodles soak up the sauce.

If it looks dry, splash in another tablespoon or two of pasta water. If it looks loose, keep tossing — it'll tighten up as the starch hydrates.

Step 5 — Finish and serve

Off the heat, scatter in the chopped parsley and a generous shower of Parmesan. Toss one more time. Taste, adjust salt and pepper, and plate immediately into warm bowls.

Top with a little more Parmesan, a drizzle of good olive oil, and eat right away. Pasta waits for no one.

That's it. Wash your hands and enjoy.
